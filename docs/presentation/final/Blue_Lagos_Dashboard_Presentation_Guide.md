# Blue Lagos Dashboard Presentation Guide

**Presenter-ready guide for a leadership audience**  
**Recommended live demonstration:** 10-12 minutes  
**Full page-by-page tour:** 25-35 minutes

This guide explains every page and major function in simple language. Text in quotation marks can be spoken naturally. Text in square brackets is an action for the presenter and should not be read aloud.

---

## The One Message

> **Blue Lagos turns community-level evidence into practical planning choices, while showing clearly what is known, what is missing, and what still needs verification.**

The presentation is not about displaying many charts. It is about showing a decision journey:

1. See the scale of the challenge.
2. Find where the pressure is concentrated.
3. Understand one community in full.
4. Identify needs that overlap.
5. Test where an intervention may reach the most people.
6. Check the quality and limits of the evidence before acting.

---

## Facts To Keep Straight

Use these figures consistently:

| Item | Safe figure and wording |
|---|---|
| Community register | **134 surveyed communities** |
| Population represented | **502,002 people**, based on recorded community estimates |
| Communities with coordinates | **93 geolocated communities** |
| Physical field visits | **Up to 18 communities physically visited** |
| Telemedicine support | **120 communities supported** |
| Survey coverage | **7 of Lagos State's 20 local governments**, with the largest concentration in Epe |

Do not use **152** for telemedicine support. Do not use the former population figure of **322,054**.

When a live page shows a changing result produced by filters or a scenario, read the current figure from the screen. Do not rely on a memorised value.

### The Three Safest Sentences

Keep these ready throughout the presentation:

> "Every distance shown is a straight-line screening distance, not a journey time."

> "The health facilities, waterways and landing points are from OpenStreetMap contributors; they are useful planning context, not official State registers or verified operational networks."

> "Where information was not recorded, the platform keeps it missing. It never changes a blank into zero."

---

## Before The Room Opens

Complete this checklist before presenting:

- Open the dashboard and confirm that the Overview loads.
- Open the Community Map once so optional map layers can load before the audience arrives.
- Open the community dossier you plan to use and rehearse its name.
- Open Shared Service Areas and Scenarios once.
- Keep `/briefing` open in a separate tab as the reliable fallback.
- Use **Deep Coast** in a bright projection environment if its contrast is clearer; use **Coastal Day** in a normally lit room.
- Increase browser zoom if figures are difficult to read from the back of the room.
- Close notifications and unrelated browser tabs.
- Do not depend on a specific external map tile loading. The community lists and figures still carry the story.

### Rehearse One Community

Choose one community that:

- Has coordinates.
- Has a meaningful but reasonably complete record.
- Illustrates at least two connected issues.
- Has a clear priority request.
- Appears in the shared-service or scenario discussion you plan to use.

Do not choose a community for dramatic effect alone. Choose one whose record you have read in advance.

# Recommended 10-12 Minute Live Demonstration

## 1. Opening Hook: From Invisible To Actionable

**Time:** 45-60 seconds  
**Page:** Overview (`/`)

### Say This

> "A community may be only a few kilometres from a health facility on a map and still be extremely difficult to reach from the water. That is the planning challenge in riverine Lagos: physical distance does not always mean practical access.
>
> Blue Lagos brings these communities into view one by one. It connects what they reported, where they are, the services mapped around them, and the interventions we may want to test. Most importantly, it keeps the evidence honest: measured facts remain facts, estimates remain estimates, and missing information stays visible."

[Pause and let the Overview settle on screen.]

> "The platform currently holds 134 surveyed community records representing 502,002 people. Ninety-three communities can be placed on the map. The others remain in every relevant table and total; they are not discarded simply because coordinates were not recorded."

### Point To

- **Communities surveyed**
- **Population represented**
- **Needs urgent attention**
- **No way out in an emergency**
- The map and the ranked list titled **Where attention is needed**

### Transition

> "The overview tells us the size of the picture. The map tells us why ordinary road-based planning is not enough."

---

## 2. Community Map: Reveal The Geography

**Time:** 90 seconds  
**Page:** Community Map (`/explorer`)

### Do This

1. Open **Community map** from the left navigation.
2. Point to how the communities follow the lagoon, creeks and coastline.
3. Turn on **Health facilities**.
4. Turn on **Jetties & landing points**.
5. Turn on **Waterways** if the map remains clear.
6. Turn on **Community concentration** last.
7. Click one community dot, but do not open its dossier yet.

### Say This

> "The dots do not follow the road network. They follow the water. That single visual explains why a normal urban service map can miss the real access problem.
>
> We can add mapped health facilities, landing points and waterways as context. These layers help us screen for gaps, but I want to be precise: they are contributor-maintained OpenStreetMap records, not official Lagos State registers, and a mapped facility is not proof that it is currently operational.
>
> The concentration layer answers a different question: where do surveyed communities gather closely enough that one shared intervention may serve several places? It is a count of surveyed communities, not a risk map."

### Make The Meaning Clear

> "The value here is not simply seeing dots. It is seeing communities, administrative responsibility and service context in the same frame."

### Transition

> "Now let us leave the statewide view and ask the question that normally gets lost in an average: what is happening in one actual community?"

---

## 3. Search And Community Dossier: Make It Human

**Time:** 90 seconds  
**Function:** Global search and Community Dossier (`/communities/[slug]`)

### Do This

1. Press `/` or click **Search communities** in the top bar.
2. Type the rehearsed community name.
3. Select the result and open its dossier.
4. Point to the community name, LGA, record completeness and need score.
5. Scroll through **People**, **Health, water and sanitation**, **Getting there and basic services**, and **What this community asked for**.

### Say This

> "At any point, a decision-maker can search for a community by name and move directly to its evidence record.
>
> This page brings the whole story together. We can see the people represented, the conditions recorded, how emergencies are moved, the state of basic services, livelihood and environmental pressures, and the community's request in its own words.
>
> Notice the completeness figure. A thin record is never made to look like a good outcome. If population or another answer was not captured, the platform says 'Not recorded'; it does not pretend the answer was zero."

[Point to the need flags.]

> "The need score is not a mysterious judgement. It simply counts how many of ten named hardship conditions were recorded. We can see the reasons, not just the rank."

### Transition

> "One record gives us the human story. The next pages show whether the same pressures repeat across several communities."

---

## 4. Health & Water Or Access: Show The Compounding Problem

**Time:** 75-90 seconds  
**Preferred page:** Health & Water (`/health`)  
**Alternative page:** Access (`/accessibility`)

Choose one of these pages in a short presentation. Show both only if the discussion calls for it.

### Health & Water Talk

[Open **Health & Water** and point to the first two issue cards.]

> "A sanitation problem is serious. No emergency way out is serious. When both land in the same community, the planning urgency changes.
>
> This page identifies where healthcare access, emergency movement, water and sanitation problems overlap. The map below adds straight-line distance to the nearest facility appearing on the open map. Red and orange mean farther from a mapped facility, not a measured travel time and not proof that no closer facility exists."

### Access Talk

[Open **Access** and point to the multiple-problems table.]

> "Access is the condition beneath every other service. A clinic cannot help if an emergency cannot reach it. A market cannot improve income if produce cannot get there reliably.
>
> This table does not hide several problems inside one label. It lists every contributing access constraint so the audience can see why a community needs attention."

### Transition

> "Once the overlapping needs are visible, the question changes from 'where is there a problem?' to 'where could one investment make the greatest difference?'"

---

## 5. Priorities: Explain The Shortlist, Not A Black Box

**Time:** 60 seconds  
**Page:** Priorities, Community priorities view (`/priorities`)

### Do This

1. Point to **Highest combined need** and **Needs urgent attention**.
2. Point to the list of ten conditions.
3. Move one weighting control in the ranking model if it is visible and rehearsed.

### Say This

> "The platform provides two different signals, and it keeps them separate.
>
> The combined need score counts ten recorded conditions equally. Five or more places a community in the highest combined-need band. 'Needs urgent attention' is different: one severe hard-stop condition can trigger it immediately.
>
> The adjustable ranking lets us ask a policy question openly. If emergency access matters more for this decision, we increase its weight and watch the order change. The platform does not make the policy choice; it shows the consequence of our choice."

### Transition

> "A shortlist tells us where need is greatest. Geography can then tell us where communities may be served together."

---

## 6. Shared Service Areas: Turn Evidence Into Options

**Time:** 90 seconds  
**Page:** Priorities, Shared service areas view (`/priorities?view=areas&type=health`)

### Do This

1. Select **Shared service areas**.
2. Keep **Health centre**, **Those with the greatest need**, 5 km grouping, 5 km reach and one facility for the rehearsed default.
3. Select one group card.
4. Point to the amber tested location, the shaded reach and the people/communities summary.
5. Optionally change **How far it reaches** once and click **Update**.

### Say This

> "This view asks whether nearby communities with a similar shortfall could share one intervention.
>
> The coloured points are communities in a group. The amber point is a location the platform tested. The shaded circle shows its straight-line reach. The totals show how many communities and how much known survey population fall inside, without double-counting.
>
> This is not a construction recommendation. It is a disciplined place to begin feasibility work. Land ownership, engineering, operating capacity, approvals and real journey time still have to be checked."

### Strong Line

> "It moves the conversation from one facility for one settlement to the reach of one well-placed investment."

### Transition

> "The suggested point is only a starting option. We can now challenge it live."

---

## 7. Scenarios: Let The Room Ask “What If?”

**Time:** 75-90 seconds  
**Page:** Scenarios (`/scenarios`)

### Do This

1. Click **Test this location** from the selected shared-service area, or open Scenarios directly.
2. Choose an intervention: health centre, water point, emergency post, solar hub, or jetty/landing.
3. Click a point on the map.
4. Move **How far should it reach?** from 5 km to another value and back.
5. Point to the changing community and population figures.

### Say This

> "This is where a question raised in the meeting can be tested in the meeting.
>
> We choose the type of intervention, test a location and set a planning radius. The platform immediately counts the communities, known population and urgent-attention communities within that distance. Move the point or change the reach and the answer changes in front of us.
>
> Nothing is saved, approved or predicted here. This is a comparison tool. Its purpose is to help us ask better questions before money is committed."

### Invite Participation

> "If there is another location you would like us to test, we can place the point there now."

If the meeting is tightly controlled, do not invite an unrehearsed interaction. Simply demonstrate one prepared comparison.

### Transition

> "A useful planning tool must do more than produce an attractive answer. It must also show how trustworthy that answer is."

---

## 8. Data Quality: Close On Trust

**Time:** 60-75 seconds  
**Page:** Data Quality (`/data-quality`)

### Say This

> "This page is one of the most important parts of the platform because it shows where the evidence is strong and where it is thin.
>
> It checks how many records loaded, how many have coordinates, which questions were answered, where boundaries and survey records differ, and where open-map context may be incomplete. Anything unusual is flagged for review rather than silently removed.
>
> Blue Lagos does not earn trust by claiming perfect data. It earns trust by making the limits inspectable."

### Closing Ask

> "We now have a community-level planning view that can take us from statewide patterns to one settlement and from recorded need to a testable intervention option.
>
> The next step is to strengthen it with authoritative State data, especially verified health facilities, jetties and navigable routes, and then use it in a defined riverine planning pilot. That would allow State agencies to compare options with better evidence and track how the picture changes over time."

### Final Line

> **"The platform does not replace judgement. It gives decision-makers a clearer place to apply it."**

# Full Page-By-Page Guide

Use this section when giving a longer tour or when a question takes the presentation to a secondary page.

## Functions Available Across The Website

### Left Navigation

**What it does:** Organises pages into Main, Themes, Planning and Reference.

**Say this:**

> "The website follows the way a planning discussion develops: first the communities, then the issues, then intervention planning, and finally the supporting evidence."

On a small screen, use the menu button to open and close the navigation. On desktop, the sidebar can be collapsed to give maps more room.

### Global Community Search

**What it does:** Opens from **Search communities**, `/`, or `Ctrl/Cmd + K`. It searches community name, LGA and senatorial district, and goes directly to a dossier.

**Say this:**

> "We do not have to search through tables. If a community is raised in the room, we can go directly to it by name."

### Filters

**What they do:** Filter by community name, local government and main need. **More filters** adds senatorial district and urgent-attention status. Active filters appear as removable labels.

**Say this:**

> "Every headline figure changes with the selection. We can narrow the whole page to one local government or one type of need and still see exactly which filter is active."

Always click **Clear all** before moving to another prepared example unless you deliberately want the filter to remain.

### Charts

**What they do:** Compare community counts by LGA. Selecting a bar filters the page to that LGA.

**Say this:**

> "The bar is not just a picture. Selecting it takes us to the communities behind it."

### Maps

**What they do:** Pan, zoom, switch between Satellite and Light backgrounds, select communities, identify LGA boundaries, reveal optional layers and reset to the Lagos view.

**Say this:**

> "Every dot can be opened. The visual pattern is always connected back to an individual community record."

### Information Buttons

**What they do:** Explain a metric in plain language and link it to its method.

**Say this:**

> "If anyone asks how a number was produced, we can open its explanation rather than asking the audience to take it on trust."

### Appearance Control

**What it does:** Switches between **Coastal Day** and **Deep Coast**. The choice is remembered by the browser.

Do not spend presentation time explaining this unless visibility requires a change.

## Page 1: Overview

**Route:** `/`  
**Plain purpose:** The opening summary of who is represented, where communities are, and which pressures stand out.

### Main Functions

- Search and filter the current view.
- Open KPI cards for the relevant detailed page.
- Explore the map and select a community.
- Review communities ranked by the number of recorded hardship conditions.
- See summary charts of people, needs and conditions.

### Best Thing To Show

Show the relationship between the headline scale, the map and **Where attention is needed**.

### Say This

> "This is the statewide front door. It shows the communities represented, the people behind the records and the pressures that deserve a closer look. The important point is that every headline can be traced down to the communities behind it."

### Audience Takeaway

The platform is a connected planning view, not a static report.

### Be Careful

Say **surveyed communities** or **communities in the register**, not every riverine community in Lagos. Population is represented population from recorded estimates, not a census total.

## Page 2: Community Map

**Route:** `/explorer`  
**Plain purpose:** Shows how communities relate to water, administrative boundaries and mapped services.

### Main Functions

- Switch between Satellite and Light map backgrounds.
- Show or hide local government boundaries.
- Adjust boundary visibility.
- Add Community concentration, Waterways, Jetties & landing points and Health facilities.
- Click an LGA boundary to identify it.
- Click a community to see a summary and open its dossier.
- Reset the map to all of Lagos.
- Review nearest mapped-service distances and boundary checks below the map.

### Say This

> "This page reveals the geography behind the need. The communities follow the lagoon and creek system, while planning responsibility follows administrative boundaries. Adding service context lets us see where a community may be physically close to Lagos but poorly connected to what it needs."

### Audience Takeaway

Riverine service planning cannot be understood from roads and LGA averages alone.

### Be Careful

The concentration hexagons show surveyed-community density, not risk. Distances are straight-line. OpenStreetMap is not an official facility, waterway or jetty register.

## Page 3: Communities

**Route:** `/communities`  
**Plain purpose:** The full searchable register of surveyed settlements.

### Main Functions

- Search and filter all community records.
- Sort by selecting column headings.
- Compare population, need and status across records.
- Open any community name for its complete dossier.
- Show search results on the map when matching communities have coordinates.
- Keep communities without coordinates in the table and totals.

### Say This

> "This is the evidence register behind the dashboard. It lets us compare communities directly and then open the record behind any result. A community without coordinates is still visible here, because lack of location data should create a follow-up task, not make the community disappear."

### Audience Takeaway

The dashboard remains accountable to named records rather than anonymous totals.

## Page 4: Community Dossier

**Route:** `/communities/[community-name]`  
**Plain purpose:** Tells the complete evidence story for one community.

### Main Functions

- See district, LGA, cluster, urgent status and record completeness.
- See the transparent need score and every condition behind it.
- Review population, households, voters, women and youth where recorded.
- Review health, sanitation, flood, emergency movement and reported hospital information.
- Review route, jetty, electricity and phone/internet conditions.
- Review livelihoods, poverty, harvest loss, erosion and preparedness.
- Read the community's priority request in the form recorded by the survey.
- Locate the community on the map.
- Compare survey context with distances to features appearing on the open map.
- See any shared health-service area associated with the community.

### Say This

> "This is where a dot becomes a place with people, conditions and a voice. We can see what was recorded, how complete the record is, what the community asked for and what calculated context sits around it. Field answers are never overwritten by the map comparison."

### Audience Takeaway

Every high-level finding can return to a specific, inspectable community record.

### Be Careful

A low need score on an incomplete record does not mean low need. It may mean too little was recorded.

## Page 5: People

**Route:** `/demographics`  
**Plain purpose:** Explains how many people are represented and how complete the demographic evidence is.

### Main Functions

- Filter demographic evidence by location, need and urgency.
- See communities with population estimates.
- See where women and youth counts were recorded.
- Compare the selected condition by LGA.
- Select a chart bar or map point to reach the communities behind the result.
- Review communities with population estimates, ordered by need.

### Say This

> "The 502,002 people represented here are the sum of the estimates communities gave. The page also shows how many communities supplied a figure, because a total without its coverage can be misleading. Missing communities are excluded from the sum and reported separately, not counted as zero."

### Audience Takeaway

Scale is visible, but the uncertainty around that scale is visible too.

### Be Careful

Do not describe the number as a census figure or a modelled population estimate.

## Page 6: Health & Water

**Route:** `/health`  
**Plain purpose:** Shows where health access, emergency movement, drinking water and sanitation pressures overlap.

### Main Functions

- Count communities with both a sanitation gap and no emergency way out.
- Count sanitation gaps in highly flood-exposed communities.
- Compare affected communities by LGA.
- Map distance bands to the nearest health facility appearing on the open map.
- Open the communities behind each result.
- Move directly to shared health-facility testing.

### Say This

> "This page helps us see compound pressure. One condition may need a sector response; several conditions in one place may require a coordinated response. It identifies those overlaps without claiming that one condition caused another."

### Audience Takeaway

Health planning should consider sanitation and emergency movement together, not in isolation.

### Be Careful

Population shown is the number of people living in affected communities, not a count of disease cases. The map does not verify whether a facility is operational.

## Page 7: Access

**Route:** `/accessibility`  
**Plain purpose:** Explains how communities connect to neighbours, health facilities, waterways and landing points.

### Main Functions

- Identify communities with no motorised medical evacuation recorded.
- Identify communities more than 5 km from another surveyed community.
- Compare health-facility distance bands by LGA.
- Review communities carrying two or more named access problems.
- Link those communities to shared emergency-service options.
- See clearly what can and cannot be measured with the current data.

### Say This

> "Access determines whether every other service is usable. This page separates what the survey observed from what the map calculates and lists the exact access problems facing each community. It does not compress them into a label we cannot explain."

### Audience Takeaway

A short map distance may still conceal a difficult or impossible real journey.

### Be Careful

Do not claim water or road travel times. Navigability, vessel availability, tides and road condition are not modelled.

## Page 8: Environment

**Route:** `/climate`  
**Plain purpose:** Shows reported flood and erosion conditions and distinguishes current evidence from unavailable environmental models.

### Main Functions

- Switch between **Flood** and **Erosion** views.
- Count communities rating flood pressure at 4 or 5 out of 5.
- Count erosion recorded as severe enough to displace people.
- Compare affected communities by LGA and on the map.
- Review missing satellite, elevation, land-cover and shoreline evidence openly.

### Say This

> "This page shows what communities reported they are experiencing now. It does not turn those reports into a climate forecast. The unavailable section is deliberate: where satellite or elevation evidence has not passed review, the platform says so instead of manufacturing a complete-looking map."

### Audience Takeaway

The platform distinguishes lived evidence from future modelling.

### Be Careful

The flood score is not flood depth, probability, mapped extent or a projection. Erosion is a field classification, not a measured shoreline-change rate.

## Page 9: Infrastructure

**Route:** `/infrastructure`  
**Plain purpose:** Finds where power, connectivity, sanitation and emergency-readiness gaps occur together.

### Main Functions

- Count communities missing two or more basic services.
- Identify communities with no emergency plan recorded.
- Compare gaps by LGA and jetty condition.
- Open the communities with the greatest stack of service deficits.

### Say This

> "Infrastructure gaps rarely arrive one at a time. This page shows where communities face several missing basics together: power, connectivity, sanitation and emergency planning. Each gap is counted separately, and a missing answer never counts as a failure."

### Audience Takeaway

Bundled service planning may be more effective than treating each deficit as a separate project.

### Be Careful

The page reflects community survey records, not a complete official inventory of built assets.

## Page 10: Livelihoods

**Route:** `/economy`  
**Plain purpose:** Connects what people do for a living with poverty depth and losses before produce reaches market.

### Main Functions

- Identify the deepest recorded poverty band.
- Identify severe catch or harvest loss before sale.
- Compare pressures by LGA and main occupation.
- Find communities requesting market, storage or transport support.

### Say This

> "For many riverine communities, the question is not only how much is produced but how much reaches a buyer. This page shows where deep poverty and severe loss of catch or harvest appear together, helping us see where storage, transport or market access may protect existing livelihoods."

### Audience Takeaway

An access or storage intervention can also be an economic intervention.

### Be Careful

Co-occurrence does not prove causation. The platform does not claim that post-harvest loss caused poverty or that one intervention will automatically remove it.

## Page 11: Priorities - Community Priorities

**Route:** `/priorities`  
**Plain purpose:** Creates an explainable shortlist of communities facing several recorded hardships.

### Main Functions

- See the equal-weight need score out of ten.
- Distinguish highest combined need from urgent-attention hard stops.
- Review the ten conditions behind the score.
- Adjust policy weights and watch the ranking change.
- Leave communities unranked where too little evidence was recorded.
- Move into shared-service planning.

### Say This

> "This is a transparent prioritisation conversation. The standard score gives every one of ten conditions one point. The adjustable view lets leaders express a different policy emphasis and immediately see the effect. It supports judgement; it does not disguise judgement as an automatic answer."

### Audience Takeaway

The shortlist can be challenged, explained and changed openly.

### Be Careful

Do not call the ranking artificial intelligence or prediction. Do not confuse **highest combined need** with **needs urgent attention**; they are separate rules.

## Page 12: Priorities - Shared Service Areas

**Route:** `/priorities?view=areas&type=health`  
**Plain purpose:** Groups nearby communities with similar gaps and screens possible locations for shared facilities.

### Main Functions

- Choose a health centre, water point, emergency post, solar hub, or jetty/landing.
- Group communities by greatest need or include all nearby communities.
- Change how close communities must be to form a group.
- Change the straight-line reach of a facility.
- Test one, two or three facilities.
- Select a group to inspect its communities, population and tested point.
- Flag groups separated by open water.
- Send a selected location into the Scenario page.

### Say This

> "This page looks for opportunities to serve communities together. It tests points on consistent criteria and reports the reach. The result is not 'build here'; the result is 'investigate this option first.'"

### Audience Takeaway

Spatial evidence can help focus feasibility work and compare investment reach.

### Be Careful

The tested point does not check land ownership, engineering suitability, approvals, capacity, cost or actual travel routes.

## Page 13: Scenarios

**Route:** `/scenarios`  
**Plain purpose:** Lets the audience test a possible intervention location and immediately see who falls within its planning reach.

### Main Functions

- Choose the intervention type.
- Choose a known community location or click any point on the map.
- Set a radius from 1 to 20 km.
- Check whether the point is in Lagos and, where relevant, on land or shoreline.
- Count communities, known population and urgent-attention communities within reach.
- Review the exact communities inside the radius.
- Compare the scenario with an area passed from Shared Service Areas.

### Say This

> "Scenarios turn discussion into a live comparison. If the proposed location changes, the communities and population within the same straight-line reach change immediately. This helps rule options in for deeper study or rule weak options out early."

### Audience Takeaway

Planning assumptions can be challenged while the decision-makers are still in the room.

### Be Careful

The circle is not a confirmed service area or beneficiary list. Nothing is saved, approved or engineered by this page.

## Page 14: SDGs

**Route:** `/sdgs`  
**Plain purpose:** Connects community evidence to relevant Sustainable Development Goals without pretending to calculate official UN indicators.

### Main Functions

- Review seven goal-aligned local measures supported by the survey.
- See the affected share only among communities that answered the underlying question.
- See how many communities did not answer.
- Map communities affected by at least one displayed measure.

### Say This

> "This page translates local evidence into the language of the SDGs. These are practical local stand-ins, not official UN indicator calculations. Each percentage shows both the communities that answered and those that did not, so missing evidence cannot create a falsely reassuring result."

### Audience Takeaway

The community evidence can support SDG reporting conversations while remaining honest about what it does and does not measure.

### Be Careful

Do not present these percentages as official UN results or State-wide SDG performance.

## Page 15: Data

**Route:** `/data`  
**Plain purpose:** Shows every dataset behind the platform, its source, status, age, coverage, processing and licence.

### Main Functions

- Check what is live, partly available or unavailable.
- Distinguish field data from external spatial layers.
- Review records, sources, dates and limitations.
- Review licensing and processing notes.
- See available GeoJSON API routes for approved technical use.

### Say This

> "This is the platform's source register. It tells us what is working, where each layer came from, how it was processed and what limitation travels with it. A source does not become authoritative simply because it appears on a polished map."

### Audience Takeaway

The dashboard has a visible evidence supply chain.

### Be Careful

Do not show API details unless a technical audience asks. For leadership, focus on ownership, currency and limitations.

## Page 16: Data Quality

**Route:** `/data-quality`  
**Plain purpose:** Makes completeness, disagreements and validation issues visible.

### Main Functions

- Compare loaded records with the database total.
- See coordinate, health, sanitation, demographic and service completeness.
- Find the least complete records.
- Map communities that have coordinates while keeping the others listed.
- Review validation notes without discarding affected records.
- Inspect OpenStreetMap extraction and coverage checks.
- Compare survey and open-map facility-distance evidence where both exist.
- Review survey-LGA and boundary-LGA disagreements.

### Say This

> "This page is the quality-control room. It shows not only what passed but what needs review. Differences are flagged, sources remain separate and incomplete records stay visible. That makes improvement possible and prevents false confidence."

### Audience Takeaway

Credibility comes from visible limitations and traceable corrections, not claims of perfection.

### Be Careful

A boundary mismatch is a review flag, not proof that the surveyor was wrong. Coastal points may fall outside simplified land polygons.

- Distinguish Field, Open map, Calculated and External evidence.
- Open a plain-language explanation for each method.
- Expand technical details only when needed.
- Review the field survey, population, boundaries and service-distance methods.
- Review priority, shared-service and scenario calculations.
- Review what is deliberately not published.
- Inspect the metric register and method history.

### Say This

> "Every important number has a method behind it. This page gives the plain explanation first and the technical detail underneath. It also records what the platform refuses to calculate when the required evidence is not available."

### Audience Takeaway

The analysis is explainable and open to scrutiny.

### Be Careful

Do not lead a general audience through every technical drawer. Open the relevant section only in response to a question.

## Page 18: Briefing

**Route:** `/briefing`  
**Plain purpose:** A focused, full-screen presentation mode with a fixed 11-scene story.

### Main Functions

- Use the on-screen arrows, keyboard arrow keys or Page Up/Page Down to move.
- Press `N` to show or hide private presenter notes.
- Use each scene's link to open the detailed live page.
- Press `Esc` to return to the main dashboard.

### Say This

> "Briefing mode gives us the same evidence in a short, controlled narrative. Each scene has a direct link to the live analysis, so we can remain concise or go deeper when a question requires it."

### Best Use

Use Briefing when time is tightly controlled, internet performance is uncertain, or the audience needs a clean story before interacting with the full dashboard.

### Be Careful

Presenter notes can cover part of the screen. Keep them closed while speaking to the audience unless the display setup makes them private. The first scene's headline says **Every riverine community, on one map**; qualify this aloud as **every surveyed community with coordinates** so it is not mistaken for complete State coverage.

# The Project Story Beyond The Dashboard

If the presentation also covers project activity, use this short bridge after the dashboard story:

> "The platform is the evidence and planning layer of a wider programme. It does not replace direct service delivery. It helps us see where support is going, what communities report and where the next intervention may have the greatest reach.
>
> Current verified project figures include up to 18 communities physically visited and telemedicine support extended to 120 communities. Those delivery figures should be presented separately from the dashboard's 134-community evidence register so that field visits, programme reach and data coverage are not mistaken for the same thing."

# Questions You Are Likely To Receive

## “Is This All Of Riverine Lagos?”

> "No. It is a strong community dataset covering 134 surveyed communities across seven local governments, with the largest concentration in Epe. It is not a complete State census, and unsurveyed places should not be interpreted as having no need."

## “Where Did The Population Come From?”

> "The population figures are estimates recorded from the communities. The represented total is 502,002. It is not a census figure and no modelled population surface has been mixed into it."

## “Why Are Only 93 Communities On The Map?”

> "Ninety-three records have usable coordinates. Communities without coordinates remain in the register and relevant totals; they are simply not plotted until their locations are verified."

## “Does This Show The Nearest Hospital?”

> "It shows the nearest health facility currently appearing in the OpenStreetMap layer. That is useful for screening, but it is not an official facility register and does not confirm that the facility is open or provides the required service."

## “How Long Would The Journey Take?”

> "Travel time is not available yet. The dashboard uses straight-line distance because a verified navigable-water and road network has not been integrated. Tides, vessel availability and route condition would be needed for a defensible journey-time model."

## “Is The Platform Telling Us Where To Build?”

> "No. It compares candidate locations using transparent criteria. A selected point is a place to begin feasibility work, not a siting decision. Ownership, engineering, approvals, cost, capacity and actual routes remain to be assessed."

## “Is This Artificial Intelligence?”

> "The current analysis uses transparent arithmetic and standard mapping methods. Every result can be traced to named inputs and a written method. The decision remains with the responsible officials."

## “Are These Flood Predictions?”

> "No. The current flood evidence is what communities reported experiencing. It is not a satellite flood extent, probability map or climate projection."

## “What Happens When Data Is Missing?”

> "It remains missing and is shown as 'Not recorded.' It does not become zero, and completeness is shown so users know how much evidence supports a result."

## “What Would Improve The Platform Most?”

> "Authoritative State facility and jetty registers, a verified navigable-water network, refreshed community field records and reviewed Earth-observation layers. Those additions would improve operational accuracy while preserving the same transparent evidence model."

## “What Decision Are You Asking Us To Make?”

> "Approve a defined pilot use case and the data-sharing connections needed to support it. The objective is to test the platform against a real planning decision, improve the underlying evidence and measure what changes."

# Phrases That Build Confidence

Use these:

- "The evidence indicates..."
- "This helps us screen options..."
- "This is a starting point for feasibility work..."
- "Among communities where this was recorded..."
- "The nearest mapped facility..."
- "Within a straight-line planning radius..."
- "The platform flags the difference; it does not overwrite the source..."
- "That figure is not available yet, and the page shows that clearly..."
- "The decision remains with the responsible officials..."

Avoid these:

- "The system knows the best place to build."
- "This is every riverine community in Lagos."
- "This is the official health facility or jetty register."
- "The map proves the facility is operational."
- "This is the travel time or actual service area."
- "Satellite data confirms the flooding."
- "The platform predicts what will happen."
- "No data means zero."
- "The AI selected these communities."

# If Something Goes Wrong Live

## A Map Background Does Not Load

Say:

> "The external background map is unavailable at the moment, but the Blue Lagos community evidence and tables remain available. Let me continue with the records behind the map."

Move to Communities, Priorities or Briefing.

## An Optional Layer Does Not Load

Say:

> "That contributor-maintained context layer is not responding. It does not affect the underlying community survey records, so I will continue with the verified register."

Do not repeatedly click the control.

## A Search Misspelling Returns Nothing

Clear the query, search by LGA, or use the Communities register. Do not improvise a community spelling in front of the audience.

## A Scenario Produces An Unexpected Result

Say:

> "This is exactly why the tool exposes the assumptions. Let us check the location, radius and known population coverage before interpreting the result."

Do not defend an output you have not checked.

# Delivery Notes

- Lead with the decision problem, not the software.
- Use one number at a time and explain why it matters.
- Pause after the map first reveals the water-based settlement pattern.
- Let one community dossier carry the human story.
- Demonstrate only one meaningful control change per page.
- Use the words visible on screen; introduce technical terms only when asked.
- Never apologise for showing missing data. Present that honesty as a strength.
- Do not read tables row by row. Select one example and explain the pattern.
- Do not rush the caveat on distances. It protects the credibility of the whole demonstration.
- End on the decision or partnership required, not on Data Quality as a technical topic.

## Final 30-Second Close

> "Blue Lagos gives us three things in one place: the voice of the community, the geography around that community and a transparent way to test planning options. It can already help us focus attention and compare possible reach. With authoritative State datasets and a defined pilot, it can become a stronger shared decision tool for Lagos' riverine corridor.
>
> The platform will not make the decision for us. It will help us make that decision with clearer evidence, fewer hidden assumptions and a direct line back to the communities affected."
